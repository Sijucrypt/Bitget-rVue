# Web Design Rules — Make No Mistakes

## Purpose

Use these rules as a quality-control checklist when designing or generating a website.

The goal is to avoid the common design choices highlighted in the reference video and produce interfaces that feel intentional, credible, polished, and usable.

## Core Rules

### 1. Do not use a purple gradient just because it is trendy
- Avoid default purple/blue gradient backgrounds as a shortcut for making a site look modern.
- Choose color from the product's identity, content, and desired emotional tone.
- If a gradient is used, it should have a clear design purpose rather than functioning as decoration.

### 2. Do not write vague hero text
- The hero section must communicate what the product/service is and why it matters.
- Avoid generic headlines that could belong to almost any website.
- Make the primary value proposition specific and immediately understandable.
- Supporting copy should clarify the headline rather than repeat empty marketing language.

### 3. Do not use fake reviews
- Never invent testimonials, customer quotes, ratings, or social proof.
- If testimonials are not available, omit the section or use clearly identified placeholders.
- Do not manufacture credibility to make a design look more complete.

### 4. Do not fake metrics
- Never fabricate numbers such as users, revenue, downloads, customers, growth percentages, or performance statistics.
- Only display metrics that are real and can be supported.
- If a metric is unavailable, do not replace it with an impressive-looking fictional number.

### 5. Do not overuse scroll animations
- Avoid turning every section into an animation.
- Do not make users wait for content to appear simply because an animation is possible.
- Animation should support hierarchy, feedback, or spatial understanding.
- Prefer subtle, purposeful motion over constant movement.
- The page should still feel coherent when animations are disabled.

### 6. Do not make every button pill-shaped
- Avoid automatically giving every button a large rounded/pill treatment.
- Button shape should fit the visual language of the interface.
- Use radius consistently across the system rather than choosing a pill shape by default.
- Primary and secondary actions can have different visual weights without relying on exaggerated rounding.

### 7. Do not rely on emoji as interface icons
- Avoid replacing proper interface icons with random emojis.
- Emojis have inconsistent rendering, visual weight, and meaning across platforms.
- Use a coherent icon system when icons are needed.
- If an emoji is deliberately part of the brand language, use it intentionally rather than as a substitute for every icon.

### 8. Give the website a proper favicon
- A website should have a recognizable favicon.
- The favicon should relate to the brand/product identity.
- Avoid leaving the browser tab with a generic or missing icon.

### 9. Include a real privacy-policy page where appropriate
- Do not treat privacy/legal pages as an afterthought.
- If the site collects personal information, analytics data, cookies, or other user data, provide the appropriate privacy information.
- Keep legal content accurate and relevant to the actual product rather than using invented claims.

### 10. Use a custom domain when the project calls for one
- For a serious public-facing product, use a proper branded domain rather than leaving the site on an obviously temporary/default deployment URL.
- The domain should be simple, memorable, and consistent with the brand.
- Do not buy a domain merely for appearance; it should support the product's identity and credibility.

### 11. Do not hide that content or assets were AI-generated
- Do not present AI-generated material as human-created work when disclosure is required or expected.
- If the product or platform requires an AI disclosure, include it accurately.
- Avoid adding a fake claim of human authorship.

### 12. Do not make a website look AI-generated through generic AI assets
- Avoid generic AI-generated imagery simply because it is easy to produce.
- Visual assets should have a clear relationship to the product.
- Prefer distinctive, intentional art direction over interchangeable AI imagery.
- Every visual element should earn its place in the design.

### 13. Do not use AI-generated copy without editing it
- AI copy often becomes generic, repetitive, exaggerated, or vague.
- Rewrite generated copy so it sounds specific to the product.
- Remove filler phrases, unnecessary adjectives, empty claims, and repetitive sections.
- Copy should communicate like a real brand, not like a generic AI landing-page template.

### 14. Avoid unnecessary cursor animations
- A fake cursor moving around the interface can quickly become distracting.
- Use cursor demonstrations only when they explain an interaction or product workflow.
- Never add cursor movement purely to make a static mockup feel busy.

## Quality-Control Checklist

Before considering a website finished, ask:

- [ ] Is the color system intentional rather than a default trendy gradient?
- [ ] Does the hero immediately explain what the product does?
- [ ] Are all testimonials genuine or clearly marked as placeholders?
- [ ] Are all metrics real and defensible?
- [ ] Is animation purposeful and restrained?
- [ ] Are button shapes part of a consistent design system?
- [ ] Are icons consistent instead of being replaced by random emojis?
- [ ] Is there a proper favicon?
- [ ] Is the required privacy information present?
- [ ] Does the domain fit the product and brand?
- [ ] Are AI-generated assets and content handled honestly?
- [ ] Has generic AI imagery been avoided where it does not add real value?
- [ ] Has AI-written copy been edited for specificity and personality?
- [ ] Are cursor effects actually useful?
- [ ] Would the website still look good and make sense with animations turned off?

## Decision Rule

**Do not add a design element merely because modern websites commonly use it.**

Every gradient, animation, rounded button, icon, metric, testimonial, image, effect, and piece of copy should have a reason to exist.

When in doubt, choose the simpler option that improves clarity, credibility, or usability.
